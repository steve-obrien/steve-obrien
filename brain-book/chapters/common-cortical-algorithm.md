## The claim

The neocortex looks remarkably similar across regions, even though different areas support vision, hearing, touch, and language. Perhaps the tissue does not begin with a separate algorithm for every sense. Perhaps structure and input teach each region what kind of world it must model.

## The ferret evidence

In developmental rewiring experiments, visual input was routed into tissue that would ordinarily process sound. The animals could still develop useful visual behaviour, and the rewired auditory cortex developed some visual-like organisation.

This is striking evidence, but it needs careful treatment. It does not show that every cortical region is identical or infinitely interchangeable.

## Questions to develop

- What did the experiments actually change and measure?
- In what sense did auditory cortex become visual?
- What was preserved, and what remained impaired?
- How strong is the case for a common cortical algorithm?
- What would such an algorithm need to do?
