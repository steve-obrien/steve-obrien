## The seed

As a dyslexic child, I received wildly different verdicts about my intelligence. Some teachers thought I lacked it. Others treated me as unusually able. The child had not changed; the evidence each adult chose to notice had.

That contradiction suggests a useful place to begin: societies rarely measure intelligence directly. They measure performances that are easier to see, compare, and reward.
