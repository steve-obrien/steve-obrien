## The puzzle

Chess, arithmetic, and formal logic once looked like the summit of intelligence. Computers became extremely capable at them. Yet a machine that can defeat a grandmaster may still struggle to cross an unfamiliar kitchen without making a dangerous mistake.

The tasks humans describe as intellectually difficult are often tidy, bounded, and easy to specify. The tasks we barely notice are open-ended, embodied, and soaked in context.

## Questions to develop

- Why do symbolic problems suit computers so well?
- How much intelligence is hidden inside ordinary perception and movement?
- What makes the physical world so hostile to brittle expert systems?
- Does the reality gap reveal a missing capability, or merely a different engineering problem?
