You do not experience the world directly.

It certainly feels as though you do. You open your eyes and there it is: a continuous world of colour, depth, objects, people, and movement. You hear a voice somewhere behind you. You feel the chair beneath your body. Reality appears to arrive fully formed through the windows of the senses.

But your brain never touches light, sound, warmth, or the surface of the chair. Sealed inside the darkness of your skull, it receives patterns of electrochemical activity. From these incomplete, delayed, and often noisy signals, it must work out what is happening beyond itself.

The world you experience is the brain's answer.

It is a model: a continuously updated virtual reality assembled inside your head. Colour is part of that model. So are the solidity of objects, the distance between you and a doorway, the location of a sound, and the feeling that the scene before you is stable even while your eyes jump rapidly from place to place.

This does not mean that the external world is imaginary. Quite the opposite. A useful model succeeds because something real constrains it. Walk confidently towards a doorway your brain has placed in the wrong position and reality will quickly correct the mistake.

Perception is therefore not a passive recording. The brain has to interpret evidence, fill gaps, draw on memory, and anticipate what is likely to happen next. Most of the time the construction is so fast and convincing that we mistake the model for the world itself. Illusions are interesting because they expose the machinery. They reveal not a broken visual system, but the assumptions a normally reliable system uses to make sense of uncertain information.

For the perceiving mind, this constructed world is reality. It is the only world available to us from the inside.

That makes perception a useful place to begin thinking about intelligence. Before an intelligent system can reason about a world, move through it, or act upon it, it needs some internal way of representing what that world contains. Intelligence begins not with reality as it is, but with a model of reality good enough to live by.
